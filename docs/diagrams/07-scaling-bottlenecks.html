Five bottlenecks, in the order they appear

The mental-model fix

Both partitioning and sharding split a logical table into N child tables. The difference is which children receive concurrent writes.

Partitioning

Children: 7 (daily) or 24 (hourly)

enqueued_at — monotonic with time

Children receiving writes now: 1

  • Today's partition is the hot one
  • Tomorrow's is empty until midnight
  • Yesterday's is read-only

Sharding

Children: N (e.g. 16)

hash(partner_id) % N — uncorrelated with time

Children receiving writes now: all N

  • Every shard takes part of the live stream
  • Concurrent partners spread across shards
  • Lock contention scales by 1/N

Hot-tail lock contention scales with concurrent writes per child, not total children. That's why finer time partitioning never helps — it just renames where the contention lives.

What partitioning actually solves

  • Cheap retention via DROP
  • Vacuum-free archive partitions
  • Backup/restore granularity
  • Parallel scans of historical ranges

What sharding solves

  • Hot-tail B-tree contention
  • Page-level lock saturation
  • Per-tenant blast radius
  • Per-partner ordering preserved
